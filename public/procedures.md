# GENERAL GUIDELINES
- Must always bring full work equipment (polo shirt, wetsuit, harness and team lycra). Wear the GUSTYKITE polo or lycra on, and take everything back home with you.
- Must always wear a wetsuit except for the first lesson.
- Must have a waterproof watch. 
- Never put a student on the water without a wetsuit. 
- Must have a long leash, enough to have them seated and still attached while the instructor stands up.
- Check the dashboard (gustykite.vikingbookings.com) to see who is your student and level, and double check with the Manager. 
- Kites should not crash more than 2 or 3 times per lesson, on land or water. If this happens, the kite control must be improved, even if this means going back on the exercise sequence.
- All exercises and lessons are to be made according the Procedures, following the exercises, sequence and timings and always:
  - DEMONSTRATE (Explain you will show how not to do it first and common mistakes, then the correct way)
  - EXPLAIN (shortly review the key points and usual mistakes) 
  - ASSIST (client makes it with instructor attached behind) 
  - CORRECT (Fix mistakes - remember to keep it simple and 2 corrections max per turn) 
- Contact reception on arrival to check what sizes are allocated to your student. Select and separate the correct sizes immediately and make sure no one else takes it.
- If you think kite/board sizes are not ideal you MUST talk to reception and instructors to make sure you can change.  
- All kites and boards must be checked for damages, leaks or cuts after every session.

# LESSON 1 (Kite control)
[Explain > Demonstrate > Assist > Correct]

Note: Check student LEVEL and review SAFETY procedures.
Warning: First lesson is not supposed to go on the water. Every exception must be approved by me.
Warning: Launch/land exercises (or if touching the sand is needed) should be done on hard clean sand close to the shore, avoiding the sticks and debris close to the dunes.
Warning: On the sand clients must ALWAYS be connected to instructor with a long leash to their back handle.

## Spot Assessment 10min
- check Wind speed/direction. Take some time to make sure it's constant. Obstacles: for the Wind (makes it turbulent) and for us (we can crash on them), Wind Window, neutral line (launch/land) , Up & Downwind concept (going up is harder = against the wind). Wind directions (side-on is the best)
00h10

## Instructor Kite setup 10min
- Unfold the kite in front of the pump, confirm strut clamps are open, connect pump leash, connect pump hose
- Pump using 2 way, finish pump using 1 way, close the caps (use only 2 fingers) 
- Remove leash, hold the LE. 
- Show the transport position. 
- Always hold the kite on the LE and, whenever possible, in the center.
00h20

## Park the kite 3min
- One hand over, other hand under, rotate the kite 90º touching the tips on the sand, and walk on the neutral line,
- Check if it's pointing straight to the wind, 
- Put some sand on top.
00h23

## Lines setup 3min
- Drop lines 2m downwind of kite, Stretch lines downwind, Drop bar on the sand Upside Down, Totally Depower and make sure lines and chicken-loop are straight. Keep center lines inside legs, Direction lines on each hand, use one hand to stretch lines to untangle. 
- Hold the 4 lines tips, pull tips close to kite, Drop direction line outside left of the kite, Drop center lines inside the kite, Drop direction line outside right of the kite.
- Connect direction lines, Connect center lines. 
- Go back to the bar and do the Preflight check (Tension direction lines to check none are over these),
- Bring bar next to the kite
- Hold the chicken loop (not on the bar) to avoid line tangling and walk straight to the side of the kite.
- Get an assistant and make sure they know what to do.
00h26

## Kite launch assist 5min
- Pick up the kite to transport position (or C position), hold on the center of the leading edge until lines are tensioned. Hold lower, under the central strut of the kite. 
- Make sure kite is not touching the sand. Make sure all lines are on equal knots and not tangled.
- Wait for the kite fabric to be full/stiff and the kite wants to move forward (pushing you). 
- Wait for pilot OK signal and release the kite immediately, walking back, away from the rider. 
Note: Instruct client that If seeing something wrong, go back to the transport position, holding the leading edge and moving towards the rider to take tension off the lines.
- The instructor launches the kite, with student assisting launching, and checks everything is correctly set up.
00h31

## First contact 20min
- Instructor with the kite, sit at sand (9 or 3) and Student on upwind side: 
- Pull line to launch kite 2m and drop slowly; Pull line to launch to 45º and drop slowly; Pull and keep pulling to keep the kite at 45º, Pull and keep pulling until 12 and release line, 
- repeat on the other side; 
- Get one hand on the bar and repeat the same exercises. 
- Then both hands and repeat exercises
00h51

## Passing kite to student 2min
- Kite on the sand (9 or 3), Student connects his leash, instructor disconnects leash, Unplug safety finger, Tell student to hold the loop and put it on his hook, (Instructor should remove tension by pulling only the depower cable right above the loop).
- The student hooks in and inserts the safety finger. Slowly pass the tension to the student. 
- The instructor says “You have it?” so the student understands this is an important command. 
- He should reply “I have it” and he is in control, with both hands on the bar from that moment only.
- Repeat opposite sequence: Instructor grabs the bar and says “I have it!”. Student should remove hands from the bar no matter what. The instructor is in control. 
- Pass it back to student
00h53

## First control 10min
- Student repeat exercises “First Contact”, use fingertips only, soft open hands, arms relaxed, don’t squeeze or pull the bar. 
Warning: A common mistake is to twist the bar and pull it sideways or up and down, and instinct to pull and grab when in alarm mode.
01h03

## Power-Depower 10min
- Explain Power-to-me, you have the power, or Power-to-kite, you pass the power to the kite (when you don’t want the power or if the kite needs power, like backstalling). 
- Repeat following exercises with full power-to-me and then again with full power-to-kite. 
- Hands together when power-to-me, hands apart when power-to-kite
  - Keep the kite stable at 2 or 10. 
  - Move the kite at the neutral line 3 to 1 or 9 to 11. 
Tip: To control the kite, pull one hand, push the other, to make the kite turn faster and avoid pulling the bar too much down to turn the kite.
01h13

## Wrong hand control 5min
- Use a single hand on both sides of the bar. 9 to 3 with left hand; 3 to 9 with right hand.
Tip: Should use the hand on normal stance (not center of bar, not on tip)
01h18

## No hands Control 5min
- With left hand, move the kite from 3 to 1 and release the bar, clap to simulate changing the board to the other hand, the kite should continue to move to 11, and regrab the bar with the right hand. 
- Same thing to the other side. 
Note: If not possible to use other side or too much wind, do 3 to 1, release (falls to 3), recover to 1, repeat.
Tip: Start on neutral line and go faster to improve skills
01h23

## Upwind walk 10min
- Kite at 45º; one hand, stand facing the wind. Light bar pressure, hold tip of bar, power to the kite. 
- Walk upwind. Stop. Change the kite to the other side and repeat exercises.
01h33

## Downwind walk 5min
- kite at 45º. Push the student to the kite so it loses tension while trying to keep it steady. 
- Walk straight downwind and pass the kite to the other side. Stop. 
- Walk to the kite, get some pull then stop suddenly.
01h38

## Balance game 5min
- Sit on the sand, carve the heels on the sand, toes up and legs as bent as possible. 
- Power to you to try to get the ass of the sand to crouch. 
- Stay in a 45º angle to the ground and use the power of the kite to balance (move the bar to adjust the power accordingly and the shoulders forward if falling back, or back if falling forward. 
- Get sensibility to stay at 45º just with the kite pull.
01h43

## Small power strokes 10min
Warning: ONLY IF CONTROL IS VERY GOOD
- Move the kite from 3 to 1. Start slowly and build up speed gradually. Use the kite to move you around. Change sides and repeat small power strokes. Sit down on the sand, speed up the power strokes (1 to 3) and drag on your feet. Shoulders MUST stay back and legs bent/crouch.
01h53

# Lesson 2 (Body-drag/Steady-pull)
[Explain > Demonstrate > Assist > Correct]

## Customer 2nd lesson meeting (by instructor)
Note: Ask how they feel physically; any doubts about the last lesson; 
Warning: Students must practice fast power relaunch before going on the first water exercises. 
Warning: The instructor must ALWAYS stay downwind of the student, especially when getting close to the shore of swimmers inside their wind window. 
Warning: When kites crash on the waves the instructor must go to the kite (not the student). Students must move to the kite to avoid ripping and strong pulls. If the waves are big, the client should immediately quick-release. 

## General lesson setup 10min
- Check from Lesson 1
00h10

## Sidewind Bodydrag 20min
- Still on sand, belly down, repeat exercise Power/Depower. - Kite at 45º control with ONE HAND. 
- On waist deep water, keep power to you, go on the knees and lay forward keeping pressure on the bar and moving straight to the kite. Release some power and move the kite to the other side. Repeat exercise with ONE HAND 
- Changing the kite side (10 or 2) several times will give some pull and power that the student must manage instinctively.  
Warning: Explain international signals (change direction; kite at 12; bodydrag; Start; Stop; Safety release)
Tip: Hold center of the bar, use wrist movement.
Tip: Body follows the kite position, use legs back to stabilize. 
00h30

## Upwind Bodydrag 20min
- Explain angle theory (wind window on the sand); Biggest mistake is trying to go too much upwind.
- Demonstrate going too much upwind. Pass The kite to the student. Use the kite as a reference point.
- Point arm and look to where you want to go. Point to the kite (speed up). Point upwind (speed down), then manage to stay in between (about 45º upwind)
Note: body stiff, slightly sideways. Feel water flow along the body.
00h50

## Board Bodydrag 20min
- Sitting down on sand repeat exercise No Hands Control, with the board changing hand to hand.
- Demonstrate board bodydrag and board recovery on sand. 
- Repeat previous Upwind Bodydrag with board
Tip: While bodydraging to recover the board only changes direction when you see the board over your back shoulder. 
Note: Show body, hand and elbow position. 
Tip: Hand can be on the board handle or strap, whatever feels better. 
Warning: Change directions and board hand often
01h10

## Board on Feet simulation 10min
- Sitting on the sand, eyes on the bar only; move the kite slowly from 11 to 1 and back (instructor assists the kite on the leader lines); Hand center of the bar; Grab handle with back hand (or strap) and get front foot in the strap then get the second foot in.
01h20

## Steady Pull 20min
- Repeat exercise Board to feet in knee deep water. 
- Stop the kite at 45º. Explain rotation control (free hand on the water; pushing the bar sideways, extend 1 side of the board); Go deeper and repeat Board to Feet in deep water. Stop the kite at 45º and power to you to start moving. 
Tip: Control rotation with legs. Stretch left leg to rotate left and vice versa. 
- 10m drag and change directions several times. When rotation is under control, do bigger drags and Power-to-me to start moving. 
Tip: Refer it's important to push the board down under the ass. 
- Same exercise and move the kite 2-1-2-1-2 and stop at 2. Depower and change directions
Tip: Watch for leg position, should be ready for waterstart.
- Move kite faster 2-12-2-12-2 and stop at 2
Tip: Push board down under the ass. Keep doing as many 8 figures as they can from 2-12 to generate enough power for waterstart.
01h40

## Bodydrag Power strokes 20min
- Strong power strokes (2-11-2) and full power (2-10-2) to master the technique and get instinctive kite control.
Note: Practice relaunch and staying calm if crashing in deep water
02h00

# Lesson 3 (Waterstart)
[Explain > Demonstrate > Assist > Correct]

Tip: Regain trust and correct Power strokes ON SOFT SAND, then on water

## General lesson setup 10min
- Check from Lesson 1
00h10

## Waterstart simulation 10min
- Sitting on the sand, the Instructor demonstrates the Power Stroke method: Toes up Carve the heels on the sand. Butt as close to feet as possible (small ball). 
- Explain ball rotation concept, not pushing the feet to stand up. 
- Move the kite 2-1-2 and faster while throwing shoulders forward until the ass is off the sand and stop the kite at 2 while keeping full power-to-me. 
Note: Stay low, toes up. Repeat both sides.
00h20

## Waterstart practice
- Small ball: After having board correctly on the feet student must adopt a compact crouch position
- Bar to Sweetspot and start moving, 
- Power stroke, 
- Sink the board and throw shoulders forward, 
- Up on the board (stay low!),
- Fall back (sit down) to stop. Repeat many times (no need to ride, just waterstart)
Note: Quick sequence guide: Kite at 45º; Bar at sweetspot; Small ball; Push board under water/ass; Power stroke (2-12-2); Board under the ass and throw shoulders forward to stand on the board 
Tip: Stay low/Don't stretch legs
Tip: Fall back/Sit down to stop immediately.
01h15

## Ride and Stop
- Stay more on top of the board and ride 5/10m
- Push the heels and sit back to slow down and stop controllably
- Repeat until waterstart and stopping are done constantly and in control
Note: Avoid making them do aggressive power strokes. Use powered enough kites so the movement can be gentle. Make sure Small ball technique is used to not waste power stroke energy.
01h35

## Keep going
Warning: This exercise is done ONLY after waterstart is mastered perfectly and student can controllably stop.
- Back on the sand to demonstrate "Engage position”: Student sits on the instructor's leg. Facing the kite, he will pull the bar to balance in a 45º body position until no pressure on the instructor's leg. Student must manage to balance using the bar trimming, shoulder movement and lowering body weight. 
Tip: Falling forward -> power to kite; shoulders back; Falling back -> power to me; shoulders forward. In any case stay low to gain balance.
- Get all the previous exercises together.
- Remember to use “Stay on the board” position until having enough speed/momentum
- When having enough speed adopt Engage Position! Student must adopt the cruising position (demonstrated previously on the sand) by letting body/shoulders fall slightly back. Full on heels weight.
Tip: If necessary go back on the sand, land the kite, stay face-to-face and connect hooks with leash to make him balance and understand how shoulders influence balance. The instructor must keep the same pull and the client shifts shoulders forward and back.
01h55

# Rentals
## Customer 1st meeting
- Greetings; ask for previous sports, kite experience
- Make sure they have PAID and signed WAIVER on the system
- Offer helmet and vest (optional for rentals)
- Check allocated gear and hand it to the client (mention he is responsible for it from now on)
- Demonstrate Safety Quick Release procedure. While they try it, explain:
Warning: Client should NOT try to Self Rescue or reactivate if the Quick Release is activated. If you are really in danger or hurt, activate the Secondary Safety on the leash to release the kite completely. We will get to you and the kite”
- They setup the gear and try the footstraps on (mention if they have any doubts, they should wait for you)
- Check bar, lines, kite pressure and attachment points.
- Launch the kite, check flight, power and untwist lines
- Lower the kite to the sand, pass the bar to client and make sure he launches properly

# Gear Maintenance
- Gear is brought back to the warehouse and washed with clean water after every class
- It can be left overnight hanging to dry but must be stored out of the Sun when it's dry.
- Periodically check safety systems are working smooth and any tear and wear on kites, bridles and bars.
- Notice and mark defected gear with visible tape
